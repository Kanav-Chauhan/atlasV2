# Managing Attrition and Retention

## 1. Purpose

This playbook gives managers practical tools to spot flight risk early, hold effective stay and retention conversations, and handle resignations. It is a draft for review by the managers' community; comments are due to People Operations by 31 October 2026.

## 2. Where we stand

| Metric | FY2024-25 | FY2025-26 | FY2026-27 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voluntary attrition (annualised) | 19.4% | 16.8% | Below 15% |
| Attrition in first 12 months of service | 24% | 21% | Below 18% |
| Regretted attrition (rating 4 or 5 leavers) | 7.1% | 6.2% | Below 5% |
| Share of leavers citing "manager" as a top-3 reason | 31% | 27% | Below 20% |

Attrition is highest at L2–L3 in Bengaluru, and among SAP S/4HANA and Kafka/streaming specialists, which are on the hot-skills list.

## 3. Early warning signs

Watch for combinations of these signals, not single events:

1. Drop in engagement in 1:1s; shorter answers; fewer ideas offered.
2. Sudden requests for leave on scattered single days (possible interviews).
3. Long time on the same project or client (more than 24 months) with no growth discussion.
4. Missed promotion, or a rating lower than the employee expected.
5. Close colleague or former manager recently left for a competitor.
6. LinkedIn profile updates and new certifications without discussion.

## 4. Flight-risk review

Each quarter, managers at L5 and above rate each direct report's flight risk in the HRMS *Talent Review* screen:

| Risk | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| High | Likely to leave in the next 6 months | Retention plan within 2 weeks, reviewed with HRBP |
| Medium | Some signals; could leave within 12 months | Stay conversation within 1 month |
| Low | No signals | Normal cadence |

Combine flight risk with impact of loss (critical / important / replaceable) to prioritise. A *high risk, critical* employee is escalated to the business unit head.

## 5. Stay conversations

A stay conversation is a 1:1 dedicated to why someone stays and what might make them leave. Hold one with every direct report at least once a year, and within 1 month for anyone rated medium or high risk.

Suggested questions:

- What keeps you at Nimbus?
- When did you last think about leaving? What triggered it?
- What would you change about your role, project or team?
- What does your next step look like, and by when?

## 6. Retention levers available to managers

| Lever | Who approves | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Project or client rotation | Delivery manager | Plan for rotation after 18–24 months on one account |
| Stretch assignment or tech-lead opportunity | Manager | Document in goals |
| Certification sponsorship | Manager | Up to ₹50,000 per year per employee |
| Flexibility (hybrid exception, shifted hours) | Manager / HRBP | Within the *Hybrid Work Policy* |
| Early promotion case | Business unit head | Only in the annual cycle, rating 4 or 5 |
| Off-cycle pay correction | Chief People Officer | From the central market correction pool; managers request, never promise |
| Retention bonus | CPO + CFO | For critical roles only; paid after 12 months with clawback |
| ESOP grant | CEO | L5 and above |

Never promise pay changes, promotions or ESOPs in a conversation. Say you will raise it, and do so with your HR Business Partner within 2 working days.

## 7. When someone resigns

1. Acknowledge the resignation in the HRMS and meet the employee within **3 working days**.
2. Understand the reason: ask, listen, do not argue.
3. Decide with your HR Business Partner whether a counter-offer is appropriate. Counter-offers are made only for regretted leavers, and only once per employee in 2 years.
4. Record in the HRMS whether a retention discussion took place and the outcome.
5. If the employee stays, agree what changes and follow up in 30 days.
6. If not, focus on a respectful exit and a good knowledge-transfer plan, as described in the *Notice Period and Exit Process*. Most of our rehires are former employees who left well.

## 8. Manager scorecard

From Q4 FY2026-27, each L5+ manager's quarterly scorecard will show team voluntary attrition, regretted attrition, 1:1 regularity and stay-conversation completion. Teams with regretted attrition above 10% (annualised) for two consecutive quarters will have a review with the HR Business Partner.
