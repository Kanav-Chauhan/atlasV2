# Privileged Access and Quarterly Access Review Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how Nimbus grants, uses and removes privileged access (administrator and production access), how break-glass accounts are controlled, and how all access is reviewed every quarter. It puts into practice the access control rules of the Information Security Policy and the Password and MFA Standard.

## 2. Definitions

- **Privileged access:** any access that can change system configuration, read other users' data or reach production. Examples: domain admin, AWS administrator roles, Kubernetes cluster-admin, database owner, firewall admin, SaaS tenant admin.
- **Admin account:** a separate named account with the prefix `adm-`, used only for privileged work.
- **Just-in-time (JIT) access:** privileged access granted for a limited time through the privileged access management (PAM) tool against a ticket.
- **Break-glass account:** an emergency account used only when normal access paths fail.

## 3. Who can get privileged access

| Access | Eligible roles | Approved by |
|---|---|---|
| NimbusOps production (AWS, EKS, RDS) | SRE engineers, on-call NimbusOps engineers (L3 and above) | Head of Infrastructure & SRE (Pradeep Joshi) |
| Corporate domain and endpoint admin | IT infrastructure team | IT Infrastructure Manager |
| Client production environments | Named engineers in the client SOW | Delivery Manager and the client |
| Security tools (SIEM, EDR, PAM) | SOC analysts | CISO (Sameer Khan) |
| Finance and HR systems admin | Named system administrators | CFO or Chief People Officer, as system owner |

Rules:

1. Privileged access is never given to interns, trainees (L1) or guest accounts.
2. Admin accounts (`adm-`) have passwords of at least **20 characters**, generated by the password manager, and use phishing-resistant MFA (hardware security key).
3. Normal user accounts never hold admin rights.
4. Production access is **just-in-time** only: maximum **8 hours** per grant, with a ticket reference. Standing (permanent) production access is not allowed, except for automated service accounts.
5. All privileged sessions to production are recorded by the PAM tool and kept for 1 year.

## 4. Requesting access

1. The user raises an access request in the service desk, naming the system, role and business reason.
2. The line manager approves, then the system owner approves (table in Section 3).
3. IT or SRE provisions the access within 1 business day; JIT eligibility is added to the PAM tool.
4. For each use, the user requests elevation in the PAM tool with a ticket number; elevation ends automatically after the approved time.

## 5. Break-glass accounts

- Break-glass accounts exist for AWS (`nops-sre-breakglass` role), the corporate identity provider and the firewalls. They are the only shared accounts allowed under the Information Security Policy and are held by SRE.
- Credentials are stored in a sealed vault entry; retrieving them requires two people (dual control) from the SRE on-call and the SOC shift lead.
- Using a break-glass account automatically raises an alert to the **24x7 SOC**, which confirms within 15 minutes that an approved incident is in progress.
- After every use, the password is **rotated within 24 hours** and the session is reviewed by the CISO's team. This is the only case where passwords are rotated on a schedule; other passwords are changed only on suspected compromise.
- Break-glass access is tested every quarter during the DR tabletop exercise.

## 6. Quarterly access review

All access to systems classified as Confidential or Restricted, and all privileged access, is reviewed **every quarter**.

| Quarter ending | Review window opens | Manager sign-off due |
|---|---|---|
| 30 June | 1 July | 15 July |
| 30 September | 1 October | 15 October |
| 31 December | 2 January | 16 January |
| 31 March | 1 April | 15 April |

Steps:

1. The SOC exports user and role lists from each system into the access review tool on the first working day of the review window.
2. Each line manager confirms ("keep") or removes ("revoke") every access of each team member within **10 business days**.
3. System owners review privileged and service accounts for their system in the same window.
4. Access not confirmed by the due date is **revoked automatically** on the next business day.
5. The SOC records results and exceptions; the completion rate is reported to the CISO. The Information Security Policy target is 100% on time.

## 7. Movers and leavers

| Event | Action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Role change (mover) | Manager reviews and removes access not needed in the new role | Within 5 business days of the change |
| Resignation accepted | Privileged and production access reviewed; no **new** privileged access granted during the notice period (60 days, or 90 days for Senior Manager and above) | On acceptance |
| Garden leave or involuntary exit | All access disabled before the employee is informed | Immediately |
| Last working day | SSO and email disabled | **By 6:00 pm on the last working day** |
| After exit | All remaining access removed, including client, SaaS and third-party accounts; admin (`adm-`) account deleted | **Within 24 hours** of the last working day |
| Contractor end date | Same as last working day | Same day |

HR sends the leaver list to IT every working day at 10:00 am. Any account of a leaver found active more than 24 hours after exit is treated as a security incident and reported under the Incident Reporting Procedure.

## 8. Records

Access requests, approvals, PAM session logs and review results are kept for **3 years** as evidence for ISO 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II audits.
