# India Strategy Outlook FY27

*House view – April 2026. For internal use by the investment team only. Not for distribution to clients or distributors.*

## 1. Summary of the house view

| Call | View |
|---|---|
| Nifty 50 year-end target (December 2026) | **26,800** |
| Nifty 50 at 31 March 2026 | 24,060 |
| Implied upside | 11.4% |
| Overweight | Banks; capital goods |
| Underweight | IT services; FMCG |
| Neutral | Automobiles, pharmaceuticals, oil and gas, metals, cement, telecom |
| Preferred market-cap tilt | Large and mid caps over small caps |

Our **Nifty 50 year-end target is 26,800**, based on 18.5x forward earnings on September 2027E Nifty EPS of ₹1,450. This is close to the 10-year average forward P/E of 18.9x. We see earnings, not re-rating, as the source of returns in FY27.

## 2. Macro backdrop

| Indicator | FY26 (actual / estimate) | FY27 (forecast) |
|---|---|---|
| Real GDP growth | 6.9% | 6.7% |
| CPI inflation (average) | 3.6% | 4.1% |
| RBI repo rate (year-end) | 5.50% | 5.50% |
| 10-year G-sec yield (year-end) | 6.32% | 6.20% |
| USD/INR (year-end) | 86.4 | 87.5 |
| Current account deficit (% of GDP) | 1.0% | 1.2% |
| Fiscal deficit – Centre (% of GDP) | 4.4% | 4.2% |

The RBI cut the repo rate by a cumulative 75 basis points in FY26 to 5.50%. With inflation expected to drift up to about 4.1% as food base effects fade, we expect the RBI to stay on hold through FY27. Liquidity in the banking system should stay in surplus, supporting credit growth.

Government capital expenditure remains the backbone of investment demand. The Union Budget FY27 raised capital outlay by 9%, with railways, defence and power transmission the largest gainers. Private capex is finally broadening, led by power, metals and electronics manufacturing.

## 3. Earnings outlook

| Metric | FY26 | FY27E | FY28E |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 EPS (₹) | 1,182 | 1,340 | 1,520 |
| EPS growth | 7.8% | 13.4% | 13.4% |
| Kaveri coverage (142 stocks) – PAT growth | 9.1% | 14.6% | 15.2% |

FY26 earnings growth was held back by margin pressure in IT services, commodity weakness and slower consumption. We expect a recovery in FY27 led by financials (loan growth of 13–14%, stable credit costs) and industrials (order backlogs at record highs).

## 4. Sector stance

| Sector | Stance | Nifty weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banks | **Overweight** | 28% | Loan growth 13%+, NIMs stabilising after rate cuts, credit cost below 0.8%, valuations below 10-year average |
| Capital goods | **Overweight** | 5% | Government capex, railways, transmission and defence; order books at 3.5–4.5x revenue |
| Automobiles | Neutral | 7% | Two-wheeler recovery offset by weak entry-level cars |
| Pharmaceuticals | Neutral | 4% | US generics pricing stable; valuations full |
| Oil and gas | Neutral | 10% | Refining margins volatile; marketing margins healthy |
| Metals and cement | Neutral | 5% | Capacity additions keep pricing in check |
| IT services | **Underweight** | 11% | Discretionary spend weak; deal conversion slow; pricing pressure from AI-led productivity pass-through |
| FMCG | **Underweight** | 8% | Volume growth stuck at 3–5%; rural recovery uneven; valuations at 45–50x |

## 5. Key themes for FY27

1. **Railways and capital goods capex** – approved as an investment theme by the Investment Committee at IC 47 on 12 March 2026. Signalling, rolling stock and electrification suppliers offer multi-year visibility. Top pick: Konkan Rail Systems Ltd (BUY).
2. **Credit cycle beneficiaries** – large private banks and well-capitalised PSU banks.
3. **Manufacturing and electronics** – beneficiaries of supply-chain diversification.
4. **Selective consumption** – premium discretionary over mass staples.

## 7. Risks to our view

| Risk | Probability | Impact on target |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp US slowdown hitting exports and FII flows | Medium | Nifty 24,000 bear case |
| Crude oil above USD 90/bbl for a sustained period | Low | -6% to -8% |
| Monsoon deficit above 10% | Medium | Hurts rural consumption and FMCG further |
| Faster-than-expected inflation forcing RBI hikes | Low | Banks de-rate |
| Upside: earlier pickup in private capex | Medium | Bull case 28,900 |

Bear case: 24,000 (16.5x). Base case: 26,800. Bull case: 28,900 (20x).

## 8. Flows

Domestic flows remain the market's anchor. Monthly SIP inflows reached ₹27,800 crore in March 2026. Foreign investors were net sellers of ₹62,000 crore of Indian equities in FY26; we assume flat FII flows in FY27. Domestic institutions bought ₹5.1 lakh crore in FY26.

## 9. Implications for Kaveri portfolios

- Bluechip and Focused Equity PMS: maintain bank overweight within the 35% scheme limit for Bluechip; add to capital goods on dips.
- Flexi Cap: continue building the railways theme up to the IC-approved limit; keep IT below benchmark.
- Small Cap: prefer quality and liquidity; avoid governance-flagged names.
