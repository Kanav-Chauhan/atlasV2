# Statutory Audit Management Letter FY26

Management letter issued by Raghavan & Mehta LLP, Chartered Accountants, statutory auditors of Kaveri Capital Advisors Pvt. Ltd., for the year ended 31 March 2026, with management's responses. The letter was received on 30 April 2026 and discussed at the Audit Committee meeting of 12 May 2026.

## 1. Auditor's Covering Remarks

1.1 We have completed our audit of the standalone financial statements of Kaveri Capital Advisors Pvt. Ltd. for the year ended 31 March 2026 and issued an **unmodified opinion** dated 25 April 2026.

1.2 During the audit we noted certain matters relating to internal financial controls which, while not material weaknesses and not affecting our opinion, we bring to management's attention. We have rated each observation:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High | Significant control gap; fix within 3 months |
| Medium | Control gap with moderate risk; fix within 6 months |
| Low | Improvement opportunity |

1.3 We identified **no material weakness** and **no significant deficiency** in internal financial controls over financial reporting. Five observations are listed below: one High, three Medium and one Low.

## 3. Detailed Observations

### ML-01: PMS performance fee computation (High)

**Observation.** Performance fees for PMS clients on the hybrid fee option (1.0% + 20% above a 10% hurdle) are computed in spreadsheets maintained by the PMS operations desk. The high-water mark is applied manually. In October 2025, fees for 12 clients were computed without the high-water mark, resulting in over-charging of ₹7.9 lakh, later refunded (management's incident ORI-FY26-06).

**Risk.** Mis-statement of revenue; client detriment; regulatory action.

**Recommendation.** Move performance-fee computation into the PMS system with automated high-water mark and hurdle logic, and introduce an independent check by Finance before invoicing.

**Management response.** Agreed. The PMS fee engine upgrade went live in UAT on 20 April 2026 and will be in production by 31 July 2026. Since November 2025, Finance re-performs every performance fee computation before invoicing (four-eyes check).

### ML-02: User access review (Medium)

**Observation.** The quarterly user access review of the fund accounting system was completed 34 days late for Q1 and 21 days late for Q3. Two leavers' accounts remained active for 9 and 12 days after their exit dates, though no activity was logged.

**Recommendation.** Automate de-provisioning from the HR system and track access reviews on the compliance calendar.

**Management response.** Agreed. Leaver de-provisioning will be linked to the HR exit workflow by 30 September 2026. Access reviews are now on the compliance calendar with due dates.

### ML-03: Invoices without purchase orders (Medium)

**Observation.** 17 invoices totalling ₹38.6 lakh, mainly for events and research subscriptions, were paid on email approval without a matched purchase order.

**Recommendation.** Enforce "no PO, no pay" for all invoices above ₹1 lakh, except a defined list of utilities and statutory payments.

**Management response.** Agreed. "No PO, no pay" will be enforced in the payables system from 1 July 2026.

### ML-04: TDS reconciliation (Medium)

**Observation.** The TDS receivable ledger was not reconciled with Form 26AS during the year. At year-end an unreconciled difference of ₹21.4 lakh remained, of which ₹6.2 lakh may not be recoverable.

**Management response.** Agreed. Monthly 26AS reconciliation started in May 2026; recoverability of the ₹6.2 lakh will be assessed and provided for in Q1 FY27 if required.

### ML-05: Branch IT asset verification (Low)

**Observation.** Laptops and network equipment at the Mumbai, New Delhi, Chennai and Pune branches were not physically verified during FY26.

**Management response.** Agreed. Verification will be completed by 31 December 2026 using barcode tagging.
