# IT Services Sector Note – Underweight Call

**Sector stance: UNDERWEIGHT (reiterated).** Growth is recovering more slowly than consensus expects and the industry faces a structural pricing headwind as clients demand that AI-led productivity gains be passed through. We see limited upside from current valuations and prefer to fund our overweight in banks and capital goods from IT services.

## 1. Our view in five points

1. **FY27 revenue growth of 4–6% in constant currency** for large-cap IT, versus consensus of 7–8%. FY26 grew 3.8%.
2. **Discretionary spending remains weak.** Clients in US banking and retail are prioritising cost take-out over new transformation projects.
3. **AI deflation.** We estimate 2–3% annual revenue deflation on application maintenance and testing work as clients use generative-AI tools, offset only partly by new AI services.
4. **Margins capped.** Wage hikes, visa costs and investment in AI capabilities limit EBIT margin expansion to 0–50 basis points.
5. **Valuations still above average.** Large-cap IT trades at 24x FY28E earnings against a 10-year average of 21x.

Book-to-bill has declined for four consecutive quarters. High utilisation supports margins in the near term but leaves no room for operating leverage when growth returns.

## 5. The AI pass-through problem

Clients increasingly write productivity commitments into renewals: in our channel checks, 6 out of 10 large renewal deals signed in the second half of FY26 included annual productivity improvements of 3–5% passed through as lower pricing. New AI-related work (data engineering, model operations, agent deployment) is growing quickly but is still under 5% of revenue for our coverage. We estimate net AI impact on FY27 revenue at -1% to -1.5%.

## 7. What would make us change our view

- Two consecutive quarters of book-to-bill above 1.35x.
- A visible pickup in US discretionary spending, especially in banking and retail.
- Valuations correcting to below 19x one-year forward earnings for large-cap IT.
- A sharp depreciation of the rupee beyond 90 per US dollar, which would add 150–200 bps to margins.

## 8. Risks to underweight call

The main upside risks are a faster US rate-cutting cycle that revives discretionary demand, and large AI-led transformation programmes that are additive rather than deflationary. Defensive rotation in a market sell-off could also favour IT stocks temporarily.
