# Stockist Credit and Collections Policy

## 1. Purpose

To set clear rules for extending credit to stockists, institutions and other trade customers in India, for collecting payments on time, and for stopping supplies and writing off bad debts. The aim is to support the FY27 domestic sales target of ₹1,520 crore while keeping domestic receivables below 38 days of sales.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all domestic trade customers billed by Sanjeevani's C&F agents in the four sales zones (West, South, North, East): stockists (distributors), super-stockists, hospital and institutional buyers, and government tender customers. Export customers are covered by the Export Credit Procedure.

## 3. Responsibility

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Chief Financial Officer (Suresh Mehta) | Owner of this policy; approves high credit limits and write-offs |
| National Sales Manager (Nikhil Shah) | Approves credit limits in the middle band; ensures the field force supports collections |
| Zonal Commercial Manager (one per zone) | Sets up customer accounts, monitors overdues, applies credit holds |

| C&F agent | Blocks invoicing for customers on credit hold in the ERP |
| Credit Control, Ahmedabad | Weekly ageing report, interest computation, legal follow-up |

## 4. Credit terms

| Customer type | Credit period | Security required |
|---|---|---|
| Stockist (distributor) | **30 days** from invoice date | Security deposit ₹2 lakh plus NACH mandate |
| Super-stockist (North-East and J&K) | 45 days | Security deposit ₹5 lakh plus NACH mandate |
| Private hospital / nursing home chain | 45 days | Post-dated cheques for 2 months' purchases |
| Government tender (central or state) | 90 days, or as per tender | Performance guarantee as per tender |
| New stockist (first 6 months) | 15 days | Security deposit ₹2 lakh plus NACH mandate |

4.1 A cash discount of **1%** of invoice value is given for payment received within 7 days of invoice.
4.2 Security deposits earn no interest and are refunded within 30 days after the account is closed and all dues are settled.

## 5. Credit limits

5.1 The credit limit of a stockist is normally set at 45 days of its average monthly purchases over the last 6 months, rounded down to the nearest ₹1 lakh.
5.2 Approval of credit limits:

| Credit limit | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹25 lakh | Zonal Sales Manager and Zonal Commercial Manager jointly |
| Above ₹25 lakh and up to ₹1 crore | National Sales Manager |
| Above ₹1 crore | Chief Financial Officer |
| Temporary increase up to 20% for 30 days (seasonal, e.g. cough syrup season Oct–Feb) | National Sales Manager |

5.3 Limits are reviewed every 6 months, in April and October.

## 6. Interest on overdue payments

6.1 Interest at **18% per annum** is charged on any amount unpaid after the credit period, from the day after the due date, after a grace period of 7 days.
6.2 Interest is billed monthly by debit note. Waiver of interest up to ₹50,000 per customer per year may be approved by the Zonal Commercial Manager; above that, by the CFO.

## 7. When supplies are stopped (credit hold)

The Zonal Commercial Manager places the customer on **credit hold** in the ERP, and the C&F agent stops all invoicing, when any of the following happens:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Any invoice is **more than 15 days overdue** (i.e. 45 days from invoice for a stockist on 30-day terms) | Supplies stopped until the overdue invoice is paid |
| Total outstanding exceeds the credit limit by more than 10% | Supplies stopped until outstanding is back within the limit |
| A cheque or NACH debit is dishonoured | Supplies stopped; future dispatches only against advance payment for 3 months |
| The customer's drug licence (Form 20B/21B) has expired | Supplies stopped until a valid licence copy is received |

7.1 Only the National Sales Manager may release a credit hold before the overdue is cleared, for a maximum of 7 days, and only once per customer per quarter.
7.2 Recall returns and expiry credit notes are not adjusted against overdue invoices without Credit Control approval.

## 8. Collection escalation

| Days past due date | Step | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1–15 | Reminder by email and SMS; field visit by MR or ASM | Zonal Commercial Manager |
| 16–30 | Credit hold; call from Zonal Sales Manager | Zonal Commercial Manager |
| 31–60 | Formal demand letter; security deposit may be adjusted | Credit Control |
| 61–90 | Legal notice through the company's advocates | Credit Control and Legal |
| Above 90 | Account closed; recovery suit or arbitration considered | CFO |

## 9. Provisioning and bad-debt write-off

9.1 Provision is made at **50%** of the amount outstanding for more than 180 days and at **100%** for more than 365 days.
9.2 A debt may be written off only after legal recovery is judged uneconomic or has failed. Approval limits:

| Write-off amount (per customer) | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹5 lakh | Chief Financial Officer |
| Above ₹5 lakh and up to ₹50 lakh | Chief Financial Officer and MD & CEO |
| Above ₹50 lakh | Audit Committee of the Board |

9.3 Total write-offs in FY2025-26 were ₹1.84 crore across 23 accounts, mainly in the East zone.

## 10. Performance indicators

| Indicator | FY2025-26 actual | FY2026-27 target |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic receivable days (DSO) | 41 days | 38 days |
| Overdue above 30 days as % of receivables | 6.2% | Below 4% |
| Customers on credit hold at month-end | 37 | Below 25 |
| Collection efficiency (collected ÷ due) | 96.5% | 98% |
