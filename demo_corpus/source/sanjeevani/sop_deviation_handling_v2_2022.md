# SOP – Deviation Handling v2 (2022)

> This version was superseded on 01 April 2025 by the current Deviation Handling SOP, version 3.0. Retained for reference only. Do not use for current deviations.

## 1. Purpose

To lay down the procedure for recording, classifying, investigating and closing deviations observed during manufacturing, packaging, testing, storage and distribution of products of Sanjeevani Pharma Ltd.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to Unit I – Ahmedabad (Changodar), Unit II – Baddi and Unit III – Vizag SEZ. It covers unplanned and planned deviations. Laboratory OOS results are handled under the separate OOS procedure.

## 3. Responsibility

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Originator | Reports the deviation to the supervisor on the same day |
| Department Head | Fills the paper Deviation Report Form (Form QA-003-F1) and submits it to QA within 2 working days |
| QA Officer | Issues the deviation number from the Deviation Logbook; proposes classification |
| QA Manager (site) | Approves classification, investigation and closure |
| Head, Quality Assurance (Corporate) | Informed of Critical deviations |

## 4. Definitions

- **Deviation**: departure from approved documents, procedures or standards.
- **Critical**: a deviation which may adversely affect the quality, safety or efficacy of the product.
- **Major**: a deviation which may affect product quality but is unlikely to affect patient safety.
- **Minor**: a deviation which does not affect product quality.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Recording

1. The deviation is recorded on Form QA-003-F1 and entered in the site Deviation Logbook.
2. The deviation number format is DEV/<Site>/<YYYY>/<NNN>.
3. Affected material is placed on hold with a yellow "UNDER TEST/HOLD" label.

### 5.2 Classification and timelines

Under this version, the timelines for informing QA management and completing the initial impact assessment were:

| Class | Head QA informed and initial assessment completed | Investigation closure |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | within 5 working days | 30 working days |
| Major | within 10 working days | 45 calendar days |
| Minor | within 15 working days | 60 calendar days |

Extensions could be approved by the site QA Manager without limit on the number of extensions.

### 5.3 Investigation

1. Root cause analysis using the 5-Why or Fishbone method at the discretion of the Department Head.
2. Impact on other batches assessed "where considered necessary".
3. Human error is an acceptable root cause; retraining of the individual is the usual corrective action.

### 5.4 Corrective and preventive action

1. CAPA proposed in the deviation form and tracked in the CAPA logbook.
2. Effectiveness of CAPA verified at the next annual product quality review (APQR).

### 5.5 Trending

Deviations are trended quarterly by site QA.

## 6. Records

| Record | Retention |
|---|---|
| Deviation Report Form QA-003-F1 | 5 years after batch expiry |
| Deviation Logbook | 5 years after last entry |
| CAPA Logbook | 5 years after last entry |

## 9. Reason for Supersession

The 2024 US FDA inspection of Unit III and internal trend reviews showed that 5/10/15-day timelines delayed escalation of quality-impacting events and allowed unlimited extensions. Effectiveness checks tied to the annual APQR were too late to confirm CAPA success. These gaps are addressed in SPL/QA/SOP-004 v3.0.
