# Glucosan-M 500 Product Monograph

*Metformin Hydrochloride Sustained-Release Tablets IP 500 mg*
*For the use of a Registered Medical Practitioner, Hospital or Laboratory only*

## 1. Composition

Each sustained-release (SR) film-coated tablet contains:

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Metformin Hydrochloride IP | 500 mg |
| Excipients | q.s. |
| Colour | Titanium Dioxide IP |

Excipients include hypromellose (release-controlling polymer), microcrystalline cellulose, povidone, colloidal silicon dioxide and magnesium stearate.

## 2. Description

White to off-white, capsule-shaped, biconvex film-coated tablets, debossed "SJ" on one side and "500" on the other. The tablet uses a hydrophilic matrix that releases metformin gradually over approximately 10 hours, allowing once-daily dosing.

## 3. Pharmacology

### 3.1 Mechanism of action
Metformin is a biguanide that lowers both basal and post-prandial plasma glucose. It decreases hepatic glucose production, reduces intestinal absorption of glucose and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake. It does not stimulate insulin secretion and therefore does not usually cause hypoglycaemia when used alone.

### 3.2 Pharmacokinetics
| Parameter | Value (SR formulation) |
|---|---|
| Time to peak (Tmax) | 4–8 hours (median ~7 hours) |
| Effect of food | Absorption increased when taken with the evening meal |
| Protein binding | Negligible |
| Metabolism | Not metabolised |
| Elimination | Excreted unchanged in urine; plasma half-life approximately 6.2 hours |

## 4. Indications

Glucosan-M 500 is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus, as monotherapy or in combination with other oral antidiabetic agents or insulin.

## 5. Dosage and administration

5.1 Tablets must be swallowed whole with water and must not be crushed, chewed or split.
5.2 Take once daily with the evening meal.

| Step | Dose |
|---|---|
| Starting dose | 500 mg once daily with the evening meal |
| Titration | Increase by 500 mg at intervals of not less than 1 week, based on fasting glucose |
| Usual maintenance | 1,000–1,500 mg once daily |
| Maximum dose | 2,000 mg once daily |

5.3 Patients switching from immediate-release metformin may start Glucosan-M at the same total daily dose, up to 2,000 mg once daily.
5.4 **Renal impairment:** assess eGFR before starting and at least annually.

| eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| ≥ 45 | No dose adjustment |
| 30–44 | Do not initiate; if already on therapy, assess benefit-risk and consider dose reduction (max 1,000 mg/day) |
| < 30 | Contraindicated |

5.5 Paediatric use: not recommended for children below 18 years (SR formulation).

## 6. Contraindications

- Severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²)
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis
- Hypersensitivity to metformin or any excipient
- Acute conditions that may alter renal function (dehydration, severe infection, shock)
- Hepatic insufficiency, acute alcohol intoxication, alcoholism

## 7. Warnings and precautions

7.1 **Lactic acidosis:** rare but serious; risk increases with renal impairment, hypoxia, sepsis, excess alcohol and hepatic impairment. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
7.2 **Iodinated contrast media:** stop Glucosan-M at the time of, or before, the imaging procedure in patients with eGFR 30–60; re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours after and restart if renal function is stable.
7.3 **Surgery:** withhold during surgery under general, spinal or epidural anaesthesia; restart not earlier than 48 hours after surgery once oral intake resumes.
7.4 **Vitamin B12:** long-term use may reduce B12 levels; monitor periodically.
7.5 Inactive tablet shell may occasionally appear in the stool; this is normal.

## 8. Adverse reactions

Most common (≥ 1 in 10): gastrointestinal effects such as nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain and loss of appetite, usually at initiation and generally milder with the SR formulation. Common: taste disturbance. Very rare: lactic acidosis, vitamin B12 deficiency, skin reactions.

## 9. Pregnancy and lactation

Insulin is generally preferred for glycaemic control in pregnancy. Use in pregnancy and breastfeeding only on the advice of the treating physician.

## 10. Overdose

Hypoglycaemia is not usually seen even at high doses, but lactic acidosis may occur. Haemodialysis is the most effective method of removing metformin and lactate.
