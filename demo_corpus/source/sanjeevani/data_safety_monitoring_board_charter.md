# Data Safety Monitoring Board Charter

## 1. Purpose

This charter establishes the independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) for the SJ-2041 Phase III trial (protocol SPL/CLN/PRT-017, CTRI/2025/03/061842) and defines its membership, responsibilities, meeting schedule, data flow, decision rules and communication with the sponsor. The DSMB protects the safety of trial participants and the scientific integrity of the trial.

## 2. Scope

Applies for the duration of the SJ-2041 Phase III trial: from the first patient randomised (April 2025) until database lock (planned November 2026). The charter is agreed by all DSMB members before the first DSMB meeting.

## 4. Responsibilities

1. Review accumulating safety data (AEs, SAEs, laboratory data, adverse events of special interest) at regular intervals.
2. Review the pre-planned interim analysis for safety and futility.
3. Make recommendations to the sponsor: continue unchanged, modify (e.g. additional monitoring, eligibility change), pause enrolment, or terminate.
4. Review trial conduct metrics: enrolment, dropout, protocol deviations, data quality.

The DSMB does **not** design the trial or take part in its day-to-day management.

## 5. Meetings

| Meeting | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Organisational meeting | Before first patient randomised (March 2025) | Charter agreement, mock report review |
| Routine safety review | Every 6 months | Blinded open session + unblinded closed session |
| Interim analysis meeting | After about 400 patients complete week 24 or discontinue | Safety, futility (conditional power), sample size check |
| Ad-hoc meeting | Within 7 days of a safety trigger (Section 7) | Specific safety concern |
| Final meeting | Before database lock | Overall safety review |

Each meeting has an **open session** (sponsor representatives present, blinded aggregate data only) and a **closed session** (DSMB members and independent statistician only, unblinded data).

## 6. Data Flow and Confidentiality

1. The sponsor's data management sends a data extract to the independent statistician 3 weeks before each meeting.
2. The independent statistician prepares the open and closed reports and delivers the closed report to DSMB members only, by encrypted portal, 7 days before the meeting.
3. Unblinded results must not be shared with the sponsor's study team, investigators or anyone outside the DSMB.
4. Closed session minutes are kept by the independent statistician and released to the sponsor only after database lock.

## 7. Safety Triggers for Ad-hoc Review

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Death considered possibly related to study drug | Any single case |
| Confirmed acute pancreatitis | 2 or more cases in total |
| Lactic acidosis | Any single case |
| ALT or AST > 3 × ULN with bilirubin > 2 × ULN (Hy's law) | Any single case |
| Severe hypoglycaemia (requiring assistance) | 5 or more cases in either arm |
| Serious hypersensitivity / angioedema | 2 or more cases |

## 8. Decision Rules

### 8.1 Futility

At the interim analysis, the DSMB may recommend stopping for futility if the conditional power for the primary endpoint (HbA1c change from baseline to week 24) is **below 20%** under the current trend.

### 8.2 Efficacy

The trial will **not** stop early for efficacy. A Haybittle-Peto boundary (p < 0.001) is applied at interim so that the final analysis uses nearly the full α of 0.05.

### 8.3 Safety

There is no formal statistical stopping rule for safety. The DSMB uses clinical judgement based on the totality of data, including imbalance in SAEs or adverse events of special interest between arms.

## 9. Communication of Recommendations

- The DSMB Chair sends a written recommendation to the Head, Clinical Development (Dr. Ananya Krishnan) within **2 working days** of each meeting.
- A recommendation to terminate or pause the trial is also sent to the Managing Director & CEO, and the sponsor must inform CDSCO and Ethics Committees within 7 days of its decision.
- The sponsor decides on the recommendation within 14 days and documents its decision. Any decision not to follow a DSMB recommendation must be justified in writing.
