# Cardivas 10 Product Monograph

*Rosuvastatin Calcium Tablets IP 10 mg*
*For the use of a Registered Medical Practitioner, Hospital or Laboratory only*

## 1. Composition

Each film-coated tablet contains:

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rosuvastatin Calcium IP equivalent to Rosuvastatin | 10 mg |
| Excipients | q.s. |
| Colour | Iron Oxide Red, Titanium Dioxide IP |

## 2. Description

Pink, round, biconvex film-coated tablets, debossed "SJ" on one side and "R10" on the other.

## 3. Pharmacology

### 3.1 Mechanism of action
Rosuvastatin is a selective, competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis. It increases hepatic LDL receptors, enhancing uptake and catabolism of LDL, and reduces hepatic VLDL synthesis.

### 3.2 Pharmacokinetics
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Time to peak (Tmax) | About 5 hours |
| Absolute bioavailability | Approximately 20% |
| Protein binding | Approximately 90% (mainly albumin) |
| Metabolism | Limited (about 10%), mainly CYP2C9 |
| Half-life | About 19 hours |
| Elimination | About 90% in faeces |

Pharmacokinetic studies show approximately two-fold higher exposure in Asian patients (including Indian patients) compared with Caucasians. This is reflected in the dosing advice below.

## 4. Indications

- Primary hypercholesterolaemia and mixed dyslipidaemia, as an adjunct to diet, when response to diet and exercise is inadequate
- Homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, as an adjunct to other lipid-lowering treatments
- Prevention of major cardiovascular events in patients at high risk of a first cardiovascular event, as an adjunct to correction of other risk factors

## 5. Dosage and administration

5.1 Take once daily at any time of day, with or without food.
5.2 Before starting, patients should be on a standard cholesterol-lowering diet that continues during treatment.

| Situation | Dose |
|---|---|
| Usual starting dose | 5 mg or 10 mg once daily |
| Asian patients (including Indian), patients with predisposing factors for myopathy | Start at 5 mg once daily |
| Dose adjustment | After 4 weeks, based on lipid levels |
| Usual maintenance | 10–20 mg once daily |
| Maximum dose | 20 mg once daily for Asian patients; higher doses only under specialist supervision |

5.3 **Renal impairment:** no adjustment for mild to moderate impairment; start at 5 mg in moderate impairment (creatinine clearance 30–60 mL/min). Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min).
5.4 **Elderly (above 70 years):** starting dose 5 mg.

## 6. Contraindications

- Active liver disease, including unexplained persistent elevation of serum transaminases above 3 × ULN
- Severe renal impairment
- Myopathy
- Concomitant ciclosporin
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and women of child-bearing potential not using effective contraception
- Hypersensitivity to rosuvastatin or excipients

## 7. Warnings and precautions

7.1 **Skeletal muscle effects:** myalgia, myopathy and, rarely, rhabdomyolysis. Advise patients to report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness. Measure CK if symptoms occur; stop if CK exceeds 5 × ULN.
7.2 **Liver:** check liver function before and 3 months after starting treatment.
7.3 **Diabetes:** statins may raise blood glucose; patients at risk should be monitored. The cardiovascular benefit outweighs this risk.
7.4 **Interactions:** use with caution with gemfibrozil (avoid combination), warfarin (monitor INR), certain HIV protease inhibitors, and antacids containing aluminium/magnesium (take Cardivas 2 hours before the antacid).

## 8. Adverse reactions

Common (1–10%): headache, dizziness, abdominal pain, constipation, nausea, myalgia, asthenia, diabetes mellitus (in predisposed patients). Uncommon: pruritus, rash. Rare: myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, pancreatitis, raised transaminases.

## 9. Pregnancy and lactation

Contraindicated. Women of child-bearing potential should use appropriate contraception. Stop if pregnancy is confirmed.

## 10. Overdose

No specific treatment. Treat symptomatically; monitor liver function and CK. Haemodialysis is unlikely to help.
