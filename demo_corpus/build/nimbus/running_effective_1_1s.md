A practical guide for Nimbus managers. It was written by Neha Gupta (Engineering Manager, NimbusOps) with input from the L5 managers' community, and reviewed by People Operations. Treat it as a playbook, not a rulebook; the minimums in section 2 are expected of every manager.

## 1. Why 1:1s matter

1:1s are the employee's meeting, not a status update. Done well, they are where problems surface early, feedback lands, and careers get built. In the FY2025-26 engagement survey, teams whose managers held regular 1:1s scored 18 points higher on "my manager cares about my growth".

## 2. Minimum expectations

| Item | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Weekly for the first 90 days of a new joiner; at least **fortnightly** for everyone else |
| Length | 30 minutes (45 minutes for the first one) |
| Format | Video on, or in person on anchor days |
| Cancellations | Reschedule within the same week; never cancel two in a row |
| Notes | Shared doc per person, visible to both; no HR records kept from 1:1 notes unless agreed |
| Span of control | If you have more than 10 direct reports, discuss with your HR Business Partner; quality drops above this |

## 3. A simple structure

Use this as a default and adapt it:

1. **Their topics first (10–15 min).** Ask: "What's on your mind?" Let silence work.
2. **Wellbeing check (2 min).** Workload, energy, anything outside work they want you to know.
3. **Feedback both ways (5 min).** One specific piece of feedback from you; ask for one for you.
4. **Growth (5 min).** Progress on goals, learning, next step in their career.
5. **Actions (3 min).** Write down who does what by when.

Do not use 1:1s to go through Jira tickets. Status belongs in stand-ups.

## 4. Good questions to ask

### 4.1 Engagement

- What part of your work this week gave you energy? What drained it?
- If you could change one thing about how our team works, what would it be?

### 4.2 Growth

- Where do you want to be in 18 months? What would make that possible?
- Which skill would you like to build next quarter? (Certification sponsorship of up to ₹50,000 per year is available; see the *Employee Benefits Guide*.)

### 4.3 Retention signals

- Is there anything that would make you consider leaving?
- How do you feel about your project and your client?

See *Managing Attrition and Retention* for what to do when the answers worry you.

## 5. Giving feedback

Use the **SBI** model: Situation, Behaviour, Impact.

Example: "In Tuesday's design review with Harbor Logistics (situation), you walked through the failure modes before they asked (behaviour). It gave their architect confidence and they approved the design in one meeting (impact)."

- Give positive feedback at least three times as often as corrective feedback.
- Corrective feedback is given privately, within 48 hours of the event, and about behaviour, not personality.
- If the same issue comes up in three 1:1s, it is a performance issue; follow the *Handling Performance Issues – PIP Guide*.

## 6. Special 1:1s

| Situation | What to do differently |
|---|---|
| First 1:1 with a new joiner | 45 minutes; cover the 30-60-90 plan and how they like to receive feedback |
| After a resignation | Within 3 working days; record whether a retention discussion happened in the HRMS |
| Return from long leave | Re-onboard: what changed, what support they need, any hybrid exception |
| After an incident (SEV1/SEV2) | Check on the people involved; blameless tone; point to the post-mortem process |
| Mid-year check-in (October) | Use the HRMS template; no ratings, but name any risk of a rating of 2 or below |
| Skip-level 1:1 | Each L5+ manager holds a skip-level with every indirect report at least once every 6 months |

## 7. Remote and hybrid tips

- Hold at least one 1:1 a month in person on an anchor day if you share an office.
- For people in another city, keep cameras on and start with 2 minutes of non-work chat.
- Do not hold 1:1s while walking between meetings or on mobile in a noisy place.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. Turning the 1:1 into a status meeting.
2. Talking more than 50% of the time.
3. Cancelling when busy, which tells the person they are the lowest priority.
4. Never writing anything down, so promises are forgotten.
5. Saving all feedback for the annual review.

## 9. Templates

The shared 1:1 notes template is in the Manager Playbooks collection. It has four headings: *Their topics*, *My topics*, *Feedback*, *Actions*. Copy it into a doc shared with each report.
